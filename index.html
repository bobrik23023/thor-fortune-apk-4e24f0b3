How the peg board works

The board is arranged as a triangle of staggered pegs, with each row one peg wider than the row above it. When a ball hits a peg, it can move left or right, then repeats that choice on every row until it reaches the bottom. The final pocket depends on the full path, and there is one more pocket than there are rows.

Because every drop is built from repeated left-or-right outcomes, the middle pockets collect more balls than the edges. Statisticians would call the shape a Galton board, and the result pattern follows a binomial curve. In plain terms, the centre fills up often, while the outer pockets are reached less frequently and usually pay more. Six words worth knowing

Pocket
The slot at the bottom of the board that holds the multiplier a ball lands on.
Rows
The number of peg lines the ball crosses; more rows mean more pockets and tighter odds.
Risk level
A preset that spreads or compresses the payout table without changing the core return very much.
House margin
The small difference between fair odds and paid odds that covers the operator’s edge.
Provably fair
A system that uses published hashes so players can verify that the round was not altered.
Variance
The size of the swings around the average result, which is what the risk control really changes.
